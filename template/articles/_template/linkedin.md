# The LinkedIn cut

Made from article.md. Not a separate draft, and not a link with one sentence
stuck on top of it.

## The shape

Roughly 150 to 300 words. The platform truncates after about three lines, so the
first three lines carry everything.

Line 1: the hook. One sentence, the reframe, no preamble. No "I am excited to
share." No "In today's world." If the first line could open anybody's post, it
is not yours.

Lines 2 and 3: the stake. Why this matters to the person reading, in their job,
this week.

The body: one idea, developed. Not a summary of the article. Take the single
strongest move from the master and give it room. A post that tries to carry the
whole article carries none of it.

Short paragraphs, one to three lines, with blank lines between them. That is a
formatting reality of the platform, not a style preference.

The close: one takeaway somebody can act on, and one honest question. Not
"thoughts?" A real question you actually want answered.

The link to the full piece goes in the first comment or at the end. Say plainly
that there is more. Do not tease it.

## What never goes in

- Emoji bullets and manufactured line breaks pretending to be a list
- Engagement bait, including fake polls and "comment X and I will send you Y"
- Claims that are not in the master, and therefore have not been through the gate
- Anything that names a struggling organization

## When it earns a carousel

When the piece has a framework with three to six steps, or a comparison. Build
it from figures/, one idea per slide, the same visual code as the article. If
the material is a straight argument, a carousel makes it worse.
